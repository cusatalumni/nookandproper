Pet Enrichment

The $30 fix for a bored indoor cat

21 October 2026 · 4 picks

Indoor season is starting. If your cat is climbing the curtains at 4am, the answer is almost never a bigger toy box — it is one thing that makes them work for food.

Why this category: cat-tree searches reached 764,193 in July, up 6.8% month on month, at an average price of $53. Boredom and behaviour framing has replaced plain toy buying — and pets are now Q4 gift recipients. One caveat: unit sales are still 8.1% below August 2025, so this is rising interest, not proven growth.

Pet supplies is one of the three fastest-growing categories on Amazon this year, and the shift inside it is specific: people stopped buying toys and started buying enrichment. A snuffle mat, a puzzle feeder, a lick mat — the category is small-ticket ($18–35) but it solves a behaviour problem, which is a far stronger purchase driver than novelty.

The mechanism is simple. A cat or dog that eats from a bowl in ninety seconds has an empty afternoon. Scatter the same food across a snuffle mat and you have bought ten minutes of focused sniffing, which is the closest thing to a natural foraging cycle an indoor animal gets. Most people report the difference in the first week — calmer evenings, less furniture damage.

The water fountain is the other quiet winner in this category. Cats are chronically under-hydrated because they do not like still water, and a stainless fountain at $30–55 is the cheapest intervention with a measurable effect on intake.

Two honest caveats. Cat-tree search interest is recovering, but unit volume is still below last year — so treat the trend as rising attention rather than a boom. And enrichment only works if you rotate it; the same mat every day becomes furniture within a fortnight.
